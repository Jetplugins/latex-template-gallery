\section{From observation to practice}
Work through these questions before checking the answers. Explain each cancellation and state where the simplified expression agrees with the original one.
\subsection*{Learning goals}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Distinguish a function value from a limiting value.
  \item Use factoring to evaluate a removable discontinuity.
  \item Recognize when the left and right limits disagree.
\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Exercises}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to3}\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$.
  \item Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(2+h)^2-4}{h}$.
  \item Let $g(x)=0$ for $x<0$ and $g(x)=1$ for $x\ge0$. Explain why $\lim_{x\to0}g(x)$ does not exist.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{examplebox}{Check your reasoning}
\textbf{1.} Factor $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$; the limit is $6$.

\textbf{2.} Expand the numerator to $4h+h^2$; the limit is $4$.

\textbf{3.} The left limit is $0$ and the right limit is $1$. A two-sided limit requires both to agree.
\end{examplebox}
\begin{remarkbox}{A useful next question}
How does the second exercise connect to the slope of a tangent? Sketch $y=x^2$ near $x=2$ and compare the slopes of nearby secants.
\end{remarkbox}
