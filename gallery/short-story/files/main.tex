The map arrived on a Thursday, folded into an envelope with no return address. It showed the city exactly as Mara remembered it, except for a small blue square where her grandmother's house had once stood.
Every other building was drawn in black. The station, the library, the corner shop with its stubborn striped awning: all of them were there, crisp and ordinary. Only the square seemed to have been added by hand.
Mara put the kettle on. She had learned that most mysteries became less urgent in the time it took water to boil. This one did not.
\begin{center}\vspace{5mm}\textasteriskcentered\quad\textasteriskcentered\quad\textasteriskcentered\vspace{5mm}\end{center}
At the library, a man was returning a book the size of a suitcase. He watched her unfold the map across the catalogue desk.
\lq\lq You have one too,\rq\rq\ he said.
Mara looked up. \lq\lq One what?\rq\rq
He pointed to the blue square. \lq\lq A place that remembers you.\rq\rq
Outside, the rain began to move across the windows. It made the city look briefly unfinished, as though someone might still decide to draw it differently.
